How does Flex interpret conditions and exceptions?Follow one preference through booking context, a rule and the matching check.+

The phrase “any seat is fine” gives Flex permission to suggest a seat change. The rest of the sentence determines when that permission applies. AI records the whole statement as a rule, with the original wording attached.

A WORKED PREFERENCE

“When I fly alone for up to six hours, any seat is fine.”

Travelling aloneANDFlight ≤ 6 hours→Any seat acceptable

For a three-hour solo journey, both conditions hold, so this rule permits a seat offer. On an eight-hour journey, this rule does not apply. Flex evaluates the traveller’s other rules, such as a long-haul aisle requirement, before considering a change.

Conditions can combine with AND, alternatives with OR, and exceptions with NOT. The evaluator returns true, false or unknown for each expression. If a required booking fact is missing, the relevant condition remains unknown and Flex can ask a targeted question.

What the engine receives

A rule records its source phrase, applicable journeys, condition, permitted change and strength. “I need an aisle” is a requirement. “I’d prefer a window” contributes a preference while the aisle requirement remains binding.

DeepSeek and Gemini adapters produce this structured interpretation. Code checks its format, source excerpts and account identifiers. Conditions support up to 8 levels, 64 nodes and 16 branches per group. Outcome conditions can make a concession depend on the complete resulting booking.

How does Flex choose between competing requests?See how shared seats, available credits and scoring determine which plans can proceed.+

A plan contains every change needed to fulfil a request. Lin’s plan includes two seat moves and an extra bag at the agreed credit amounts. The engine checks the resulting seating for all three companions, including Mia and Jamie, whose seats stay in place.

Several valid plans may need the same seat or draw on the same funds. Flex therefore chooses a compatible set of plans. Every selected plan must fit the seats, service capacity, credit reservations and airline reward budget available to that set.

Which combination is preferred?

The policy assigns a benefit score to each plan. Flex first seeks the combination with the highest total score. If scores tie, it prefers the greater operational value after service costs, risk and the stressed cost of issuing credits. A further tie favours lower new credit issuance. The passenger prices already accepted remain fixed during this comparison.

How is the search kept manageable?

The solver explores selecting or excluding each plan. Selecting A removes B from that branch because they need the same seat. For every branch, it calculates an optimistic bound on the score the remaining plans could achieve. A branch that cannot improve the best result found can be skipped. This is branch-and-bound.

When the search finishes, the result is the best combination among the generated plans. If the work limit is reached, the result includes the best feasible combination found and an upper bound on how much its score could improve.

The precise model

Each complete candidate has a binary selection variable. Resource demand must fit remaining capacity, wallet debits must fit available funds and each request’s earmark, and conflicting allocation versions exclude concurrent plans. Resource releases in an uncommitted plan become available after that plan completes.

The current generator searches seat cycles and chains involving up to four travellers and bundles of up to three parts. It records candidate and work limits separately from selection completion. Each plan carries its booking dependencies, quote revision and expiry, which are checked again before reservation and execution.

What happens after Daniel accepts the seat change?Follow the booking updates and credit entries, including a booking response that gets interrupted.+

Lin’s published request records his approval of the seats and bag for 360 credits, together with a deadline. Flex reserves those credits for the exact requested outcome. His other 40 credits remain available. An unmatched request closes at its deadline and releases its reservation.

When the engine finds the seat swap, Flex checks the current booking and reserves the required resources. Daniel then accepts his move to 34C for 160 credits. Lin’s published approval covers his side of the agreement.

Flex sends the seat and baggage updates to their booking services and verifies the responses. Once the requested seats and bag are confirmed, it records one completed credit transaction. Lin pays 360 credits, Daniel receives 160, and the baggage product uses 200.

A booking update can finish before its response arrives.

In the recovery example below, the seat service applies the exchange and its response is lost. Flex checks the booking records to establish what happened, completes the remaining baggage update and records the credits once.

How retries preserve the result

Flex records completed booking changes and checks those records during recovery. It then resumes the remaining work. Stable command identities prevent repeated commands from applying twice, and each arrangement has one final credit entry.

How are the results checked?Compare solver output with reference solutions and replay the same sequence of events.+

For small markets, an independent reference solver enumerates every subset of candidate plans. Its best feasible result is compared with the matching engine’s result. This checks whether the selection respects the constraints and chooses the right objective value.

The table below shows recorded selection runs. A candidate is one proposed complete plan. Nodes visited measures the search work. The result indicates whether the search finished comparing the supplied plans or returned its best feasible choice at the limit.

Completed searches prove the selection within their supplied candidate set. Candidate generation uses separate limits of four-traveller cycles and chains, three-part bundles, 192 candidates and a 20,000-step work budget. Each generated-market result records the explored domain and why generation stopped.

Transaction tests exercise another part of the system. They run 48 fixed seeds with 440 scheduled commands, then replay the same commands and compare the intermediate states and credit entries. These sequences cover expiry, concurrent changes, retries and interrupted booking writes.

The timings are local synthetic measurements, including runtime and initialization effects. A live evaluation would compare request fulfilment, waiting time, disruption, service cost and credit liability with the same market operated without Flex.
